## Overview
Narendra Modi has served as the Prime Minister of India since May 2014, steering the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) to three consecutive National Democratic Alliance (NDA) governments and positioning himself as a central figure in India’s contemporary political landscape.[^1][^2]

## Fast Facts
- **Born:** 17 September 1950, Vadnagar, Gujarat, India.[^1]
- **Education:** Master of Arts in political science, Gujarat University.[^1]
- **Political affiliations:** Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS); Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP).[^1]
- **Current role:** Prime Minister of India (2014–present); Member of Parliament for Varanasi (2014–present).[^2][^4]
- **Notable reputation:** Projects centred on development, welfare delivery, and Hindu nationalist messaging, coupled with high approval ratings and polarising debates about democratic norms.[^2][^13]

## Political Rise
Modi joined the RSS in the early 1970s, transitioned to the BJP in 1987, and became Gujarat’s chief minister in 2001 after organisational roles at the national level.[^1] He led the BJP to emphatic state victories in 2002, 2007, and 2012, building the "Gujarat model" narrative that propelled the party’s national campaign in 2014.[^1][^6] As prime minister, Modi secured re-election in 2019 and, despite the BJP losing its solo majority, returned for a third term in 2024 atop an NDA coalition majority of 293 Lok Sabha seats.[^2][^3]

## Governance Priorities and Flagship Programmes
- **Social welfare and inclusion:** Modi’s administration highlights the Jan Bhagidari ethos (“Sabka Saath, Sabka Vikas, Sabka Vishwas, Sabka Prayas”), scaling schemes such as Jal Jeevan Mission (rural tap water), Saubhagya (rural electrification), PM Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana (free food grains), and agricultural support initiatives like NaMo Drone Didi and PM-Kisan Samman Nidhi.[^6]
- **Economic and industrial policy:** 2024 reforms introduced the Employment-Linked Incentive Scheme for job creation, a nationwide rooftop solar subsidy to add 30 GW, and the abolition of the angel tax for all investors to stimulate startup investment.[^7]
- **Human capital, research, and energy security:** In May 2025, the cabinet approved upgrades to Industrial Training Institutes, expansion of Indian Institutes of Technology, and revisions to the SHAKTI coal allocation policy to bolster skilled labour and domestic energy supply.[^8]

## 2024–2025 Strategic Developments
- **Third-term agenda:** Following the 2024 general election, Modi pledged reforms focusing on defence production, employment, exports, and farmer welfare as he adjusted to governing with coalition partners.[^2][^3]
- **Independence Day 2025 announcements:** Modi launched the ₹1 lakh crore Pradhan Mantri Viksit Bharat Rojgar Yojana, promising ₹15,000 incentives for first-time private-sector hires, and called for mission-mode semiconductor manufacturing, GST simplification by October, and expanded nuclear energy capacity.[^9][^10][^11]
- **Security posture:** The government announced the Sudarshan Chakra integrated defence shield and cited the success of “Operation Sindoor,” underscoring a readiness to counter cross-border drone and missile threats.[^11]
- **Demographic and migration policy:** Modi unveiled a high-powered demographic mission targeting illegal infiltration, framed as essential for safeguarding livelihoods and internal security.[^12]

## Criticism and Challenges
Modi’s tenure draws scrutiny for democratic backsliding concerns, including pressure on institutions, media, and religious minorities, alongside recurring debate over his handling of the 2002 Gujarat riots.[^2][^13] Analysts note that the BJP’s reduced 2024 seat share may require greater consensus-building within the NDA, while persistent issues such as unemployment, inequality, and community tensions remain salient.[^2][^3][^13]
